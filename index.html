More than 6,400
orphaned wells in Louisiana

Across Louisiana, thousands of oil and gas wells sit abandoned, left behind by operators with no one responsible for plugging them or cleaning up contamination.

Each dot on this map is one of Louisiana’s 6,400 orphan wells.

1,947
orphaned since January 2025

In roughly last year, nearly 2,000 wells were added to the orphan list. Wells are being added to the orphaned well list faster than the state can plug them.

The red dots show wells orphaned since January 2025. The state has more orphaned wells now than it ever has before, according ot the Department of Conservation and Energy.

TBR Operating Company

503
orphan wells

One company, TBR Operating, went bankrupt last year and orphaned more than 500 wells in Union and Claiborne Parishes in north Louisiana.

Cox Operating, L.L.C.

350
orphan wells

Another company, Cox Operating, orphaned more than 300 wells when it went bankrupt last year. The wells it owned are scattered across the coastal parishes of south Louisiana.

Together, TBR Operating and Cox Operating account for about one in every eight orphan wells in Louisiana. Plugging a single well can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. And taxpayers are left with the bill.

Source: Louisiana Dept. of Natural Resources, Office of Conservation. Data as of March 30, 2026.
